# Sankofa Twi — Narrative Blueprint

> A story-driven wrapper for the existing app. Nothing here requires new
> systems — it re-frames what's already built (the map, the four family guides,
> celebrations, Anansesɛm, day names, Adinkra) into one emotional through-line:
> **coming home to the mother tongue.**
> Draft v1 — grounded in the current feature set.

---

## 0. The spine: Sankofa

The whole app already carries its thesis in its name. **Sankofa** — *"Se wo were
fi na wosankofa a yenkyi"* / "it is not taboo to go back for what you have
forgotten." The gold Adinkra heart-spiral in our logo **is** the promise.

So the narrative isn't a bolt-on fantasy plot — it's the literal emotional truth
of the user: *you are going back to fetch something that is yours.* Every system
we've built maps onto that one sentence. The job of this blueprint is to make the
learner **feel** it at the right moments, in the family's voice, without ever
slowing the lesson loop.

**One-line premise:** *You've come home to reclaim your mother tongue, and your
family is here to walk you back through it — landmark by landmark, story by story.*

---

## 1. The Narrative Arc — "The Homecoming"

Frame the **map as a return journey across Ghana**, from the coast inland, where
each region is a *chapter of remembering* and each landmark is a *memory
reclaimed.* The four family members aren't mascots decorating a game board — they
are your **guides home**, each with a role that already matches their title.

### The four guides (already built — just give them their job)

| Character | Title (existing) | Narrative role | Voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Super Auntie** | The Trendsetter | Your entry point — makes Twi feel *alive and cool*, not a museum piece | Playful, modern, slang-fluent, hype |
| **Super Uncle** | The Cool Head | Steadies you when it's hard; the encourager | Calm, reassuring, "you've got this" |
| **Super Grandma** | The Wise One | Meaning-keeper — proverbs, Adinkra, the *why* | Warm, unhurried, deep |
| **Super Grandpa** | The Storyteller | Keeper of Anansesɛm and history | Twinkling, theatrical, elder-by-the-fire |

The learner **chooses / unlocks** who walks with them (already true via the
Compound + shard unlocks). That choice becomes a *relationship*, not a skin.

### The chapters (map = journey, landmarks = memories)

Use the real landmarks as the emotional beats of the arc. Order them as a
coast→inland homecoming:

1. **Osu Night Market (Accra)** — *Arrival.* The noise, the greetings, the
   everyday. First words: *Akwaaba, Wo ho te sɛn.* Auntie meets you here.
2. **Labadi Beach / Coco Beach (Accra coast)** — *Finding your feet.* Numbers,
   family, food — the language of belonging. Uncle keeps you steady.
3. **Kakum Canopy Walk (Central Region)** — *Rising.* You're above the canopy
   now; you can see how far you've come. A perspective / confidence beat.
4. **Elmina Castle (Central Region)** — *The solemn memory.* Handle with gravity:
   the transatlantic slave trade, remembrance, return. This is the emotional
   core of "reclaiming roots" — the ancestors who were taken; you, coming back.
   Grandma holds this chapter.
5. **Mole National Park (Savannah Region)** — *The deep interior.* The oldest
   stories, the land itself. Grandpa's Anansesɛm belong here.

> **Why this works:** the map already *gates* landmarks behind boss lessons and
> plays a **checkpoint-travel cinematic** on arrival. That cinematic is the
> chapter break. We just give each arrival a *line of meaning* instead of only a
> "new landmark" label.

### The arc in one breath
**Arrive → belong → rise → remember → carry it forward.** By the last landmark
the learner isn't "finishing a course" — they're **fluent enough to be told a
folktale in Twi and understand it.** That's the payoff we're building toward
(and it's already literally the folklore reading module).

## 2. Contextual Story Beats — where narrative appears in the daily flow

Rule of thumb: **narrative rides on transitions we already have.** Never add a
screen the user must dismiss to reach a lesson. Beats live in moments of *waiting,
winning, or arriving.*

### A. Daily open — a family greeting (home header)
The header already greets by name and time of day (*Maakye/Maaha/Maadwo,
[Name]*). Layer **one rotating line from your equipped guide** beneath it.
- Auntie: *"Maaha, Ama! Ready to shine today? Yɛnkɔ!"*
- Uncle: *"Take it easy — one lesson at a time. You've got this."*
- Grandma: *"Every word you learn, an ancestor smiles."*
- Grandpa: *"Aane, aane… sit, we have work to do. And maybe a story after."*

### B. Before a lesson — a tiny "why" (optional, first lesson of a category only)
When a new category opens, a **one-sentence cultural frame** from Grandma:
- Greetings: *"In Akan, we don't rush past a person — we ask how they are. Learn
  this, and you can enter any room in Ghana."*
- Day names: *"Before your job or your town, we name you by the day you were
  born. Let's find yours."*

### C. Mid-lesson streaks — micro-encouragement (Uncle)
On a 3-in-a-row correct combo (the "wisdom keys" moment already exists), a quick
Uncle line: *"Ɛyɛ! Now you're moving."* Keep it to a flash, no dismiss.

### D. Lesson clear — the celebration *is* the beat (already built!)
The family celebration video already fires on every pass. Give it **spoken-style
copy** tied to the character:
- Auntie: *"Ayɛkoo! You did that. 💅"*
- Grandpa: *"Ayɛkoo, my child. The ancestors heard you."*
(One headline per character, rotating — see §4.)

### E. Boss / Act clear — the arrival monologue (checkpoint cinematic)
The travel cinematic lands on a landmark. Replace the bare "NEW LANDMARK" with a
**2-line arrival beat** from the chapter's guide. Example (Elmina, Grandma):
> *"We've reached Elmina. These walls remember. You came back — that is the
> whole point of Sankofa. Breathe, then let's go on."*

### F. Story Stop — Anansesɛm framing (already built!)
The folklore reader already opens with the Storyteller by the fire. Add
**Grandpa's one-line intro** to each tale and a **one-line moral** at the end
(the proverb it teaches). This is where "cultural lore" lives most naturally.

### G. Streak risk — the family calls you back (notification / re-open)
If the streak is at risk, the message comes from a *person*, not the app:
- *"Grandma noticed you didn't sit with us today. Two minutes keeps the cloth
  growing. 🧶"*

> **Cadence guard:** at most **one** narrative line per screen, and never two
> "read me" beats back to back. Silence is part of the pacing.

## 3. Onboarding / Welcome Storyline — the first 90 seconds

Goal: by the end of onboarding the user has (a) felt the Sankofa promise, (b)
met a guide, and (c) **received their Akan day-name** — a piece of identity they
didn't have 90 seconds ago. That day-name is the hook: *the app gave me a name
that's mine.*

**Beat 1 — the cold open (full-screen, velvet + gold spiral):**
> *"Some things we carry. Some things we go back for."*
> *(pause)* *"Akwaaba — welcome home."*

**Beat 2 — meet Auntie (the friendliest door in):**
> Auntie walks up (reuse the celebration/idle art): *"Charlie! You made it. I'm
> your Auntie — I'll get you talking like family in no time. First things
> first…"*

**Beat 3 — claim your name (reuse the Day-Name tool + the new Profile flow):**
> *"When were you born? In Akan, that day gives you your soul-name — your* kra
> din*."* → user picks DOB → reveal: *"You're **Kwabena** — born on a Tuesday,
> full of fire. Nice to finally meet you properly."* → **"Use as my name."**

**Beat 4 — the first word, immediately:**
> Straight into **Akwaaba / Wo ho te sɛn** as lesson one — win in under a minute,
> trigger the first family celebration. Retention is won or lost here.

**Beat 5 — the promise on the map:**
> Pan the map: *"This is the journey home — from the market in Accra all the way
> to the old stories inland. We walk it together. Yɛnkɔ — let's go."*

> Everything above reuses existing art and the day-name logic. No new engine —
> it's sequencing + copy.

## 4. Engagement Hooks — turning celebrations & milestones into retention

The celebration animations are your **strongest retention asset** — make them
*earned, varied, and worth coming back for.*

### 1. Rotating celebration lines (variable reward)
Each character has a small pool of headlines; the app rotates so the win never
feels canned. (Draft pools below.) Variable copy = dopamine.

### 2. "Your family reacts" — relationship over score
Frame streak/level/mastery milestones as the **family being proud**, not a number
going up:
- Level up → the guide who fits the milestone appears: *"Level 5! Uncle's
  impressed — and he doesn't impress easy."*
- 7-day streak → Grandma: *"A week. In my day we called that discipline. I'm
  proud of you."*

### 3. Milestone = a new guide joins (already the unlock system)
Re-frame avatar unlocks as **family members choosing to walk with you** once
you've earned it: *"Grandpa has decided you're ready for the old stories. He's
joined your journey."* (This is exactly the Level/Streak/Mastery + shard unlock —
just narrated.)

### 4. The daily "one more" — a story as the reward
Tie **Anansesɛm** to consistency: *"Keep your streak this week and Grandpa opens
a new tale on Sunday."* Stories are a renewable, cheap, on-brand reward that also
teaches.

### 5. Weekly Rally as "the family name on the board"
Re-frame the weekly leaderboard from competition to **representing your people**:
*"You're carrying the family this week — 3rd on the board. Yɛn kɔ!"*

### 6. Streak-freeze framing (loss aversion, kindly)
The kente cloth you weave by streak is already a beautiful sunk-cost hook. Voice
it: *"Don't let the cloth unravel — Grandma saved you a freeze."*

### Draft celebration pools (drop-in copy)

**Auntie (Trendsetter):**
- "Ayɛkoo! You ate that up. 💅"
- "Twi is giving *fluent* — keep going!"
- "That's my star. Yɛnkɔ!"

**Uncle (Cool Head):**
- "Ɛyɛ. Steady and strong — that's how it's done."
- "See? You had it all along."
- "One more step home. I'm right here."

**Grandma (Wise One):**
- "Ayɛkoo. Every word is a root re-planted."
- "The ancestors are smiling, me ba."
- "Wisdom looks good on you."

**Grandpa (Storyteller):**
- "Ayɛkoo! Now *that* deserves a story."
- "You spoke it true. Kwaku Ananse would nod."
- "Home is getting closer, my child."

## 5. Implementation notes (low-lift → higher-lift)

**Low-lift (copy + sequencing on existing screens):**
- Family greeting line on the home header (rotates by equipped guide).
- Rotating celebration headlines (swap the single "Ayɛkoo!" for a per-character
  pool).
- Arrival monologue on the checkpoint cinematic (2 lines per landmark).
- Anansesɛm intro + closing proverb (per tale).
- Onboarding copy beats (reuse day-name + celebration art).

**Medium-lift:**
- A tiny `NarrativeBeat` model (character id + line pool + trigger) so copy lives
  in data, not scattered in widgets — one place to write/localise all lines.
- "Family reacts" variants keyed to level/streak/mastery milestones.
- Streak-risk notification copy in the family's voice.

**Higher-lift (later):**
- Voiced lines (native Twi VO) for the arrival monologues and celebrations —
  huge emotional payoff, but it's recording + audio wiring.
- Per-region theming of the map (already flagged as a shard sink) so each chapter
  *looks* distinct as you cross it.

**Guardrails:**
- One beat per screen; never gate a lesson behind story.
- Every Twi line must be accurate and native-checked before ship.
- Handle **Elmina** with real cultural gravity — no gamified confetti on that
  arrival; it's remembrance, not a "win."

---

## 6. The measurable bet

If we do §3 (onboarding) and §4.1–4.4 (rotating celebrations + story-as-reward)
first, the hypotheses to watch:
- **D1 retention** ↑ from the day-name hook + sub-60s first win.
- **D7 retention** ↑ from "streak → Sunday story" and family-reaction milestones.
- **Session depth** ↑ from "one more" story pulls.

Start with copy on the moments we already interrupt for (celebration, arrival,
open). It's the cheapest possible way to turn a good app into a *felt* one.
